Display and Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) on MSI Claw

CSW PN8007QB1-2 panel, DisplayID 2.0 descriptor, 48..120 Hz vs 60..120 Hz ranges, LFC behavior, and 6500K calibration.

Hardware: MSI Claw 8 AI+, Claw 7 AI+, Claw A1M
Panel: 8" IPS 1920x1200 120Hz VRR
Reading Time: 5 minutes

1. CSW PN8007QB1-2 Panel and DisplayID 2.0

The MSI Claw 8 AI+ uses an 8" IPS panel at 1920x1200 (16:10, 120 Hz). Its 48..120 Hz VRR range is stored in the TCON's DisplayID 2.0 descriptor over eDP, bypassing legacy EDID 1.4 Range Limits restrictions.

2. Hardware vs Active Ranges (48..120 Hz vs 60..120 Hz)

Panel specs specify 48..120 Hz, yet Windows 11 and the stock Intel driver operate within 60..120 Hz by default.

Parameter Panel Spec Stock Intel (COMPATIBLE) EXCELLENT Profile (ArcSyncSwitch)
VRR Range 48..120 Hz 60..120 Hz 48..120 Hz + LFC (below 48 FPS)
Source DisplayID 2.0 (TCON) Intel Arc driver + Windows DRR ControlLib.dll (native 1x VRR)
Behavior Hardware ceiling Flicker-free 2D desktop; early LFC below 60 FPS Smooth 1x VRR pacing across 48..60 FPS in games
  • 2D Flicker Protection: Dropping below 60 Hz on static bright backgrounds causes slight IPS capacitor voltage drift and faint flicker, so the driver locks the desktop floor at 60 Hz.
  • Windows 11 DRR: On the desktop, Windows idles at 60 Hz for battery efficiency and ramps to 120 Hz on cursor movement, scrolling, or animations.
  • Unlocking 48 Hz in Games: The stock COMPATIBLE profile keeps 1x VRR clamped at 60 Hz even in 3D titles. Switching to EXCELLENT via ArcSyncSwitch unlocks the full 48..120 Hz hardware floor.

3. LFC Physics and Why 30 Hz VRR Mods Fail

Lowering the VRR floor to 30 or 36 Hz via CRU breaks LFC scaling and causes visible IPS strobing.

Low Framerate Compensation (LFC)

LFC requires at least a 2x ratio between maximum and minimum refresh rates: 

LFC Ratio = Max / Min >= 2.0  |  120 Hz / 48 Hz = 2.5x

Below 48 FPS (with 48..120 Hz active), the driver doubles or triples scanouts cleanly: 35 FPS runs at 70 Hz (2x), 30 FPS at 60 Hz (2x) or 90 Hz (3x), and 40 FPS at 80 Hz (2x).

VRR Envelope, 116 FPS Cap, and Micro-Stutter Fix:

Upper Cap (116..117 FPS): Hitting 120.0 FPS disengages VRR, triggering tearing (V-Sync off) or buffering delay (V-Sync on). Capping at 116 FPS leaves ~0.3 ms of headroom to stay locked inside VRR with minimal lag.

Stock Profile (COMPATIBLE) vs ArcSyncSwitch: In the stock Intel driver profile (COMPATIBLE), the 1x VRR floor sits at 60 Hz, pushing the driver into LFC below ~60 FPS and causing pacing flutter near the boundary. ArcSyncSwitch calls ControlLib.dll to enable the native EXCELLENT profile (48..120 Hz), moving the LFC threshold down to 48 Hz so 50..60 FPS caps run smoothly, custom 16:10 modes stay intact, and a logon task restores it at sign-in.

Statistics Overlays: Even under 48..120 Hz, on-screen performance telemetry overlays can break smoothness at 60 FPS. Disable any stats overlay if micro-stutter appears around 60 FPS.

Penalties of Forcing 30 Hz via CRU:
  • Subpixel Strobing: at 30 Hz (~33.3 ms dwell), IPS cells lose charge before the next refresh, producing severe flicker.
  • Broken LFC Multipliers: a 30..120 Hz (4.0x) range causes irregular 2x/3x/4x switching and pacing stutter.
  • TCON Blackouts: running below the 48 Hz Pixel Clock floor causes PLL phase loss and 1-2 second black screens.

120 Hz + VRR vs Fixed 48 Hz

  • 120 Hz + VRR: for demanding titles with fluctuating framerates dipping below 48 FPS (LFC keeps motion smooth).
  • 48 Hz + V-Sync: for maximum battery life in games holding a steady 48 FPS; lowers eDP controller draw and caps GPU output at 48 frames.

4. Custom 1600x1000 and 1536x960 Without Losing VRR

handheld-resolution-pack adds 1600x1000 (1.20x) and 1536x960 (1.25x) via Windows Registry EDID_OVERRIDE entries without modifying DisplayID 2.0, keeping 48..120 Hz TCON timing intact.

In Intel Graphics Command Center (Display), set Scaling Mode to GPU Scaling and Scaling Method to Preserve Aspect Ratio to keep true 16:10 geometry.

GPU load savings and upscaler setups are covered in Game Tuning: Render Resolutions →.

5. 6500K Color Calibration (Optional)

Out of the box, the MSI Claw 8 AI+ white point measures cool at ~7852K (Notebookcheck review). The ICC profile calibrates white balance to sRGB 6500K (D65).

  1. Download PN8007QB1_2_cal.icm (26 KB).
  2. Open colorcpl.exe, select the internal display, and enable "Use my settings for this device".
  3. Click "Add", choose the .icm file, and click "Set as Default Profile".

In windowed and borderless fullscreen games, Windows 11 retains the profile via the built-in Calibration Loader task with no third-party loaders needed.
